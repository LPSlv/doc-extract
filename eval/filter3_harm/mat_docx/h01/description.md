**Figure 1 (arc / chord diagram, two panels a and b).** Caption: "Figure 1: A diagrammatic representation of Fourier variables."

**Panel (a) — left.** A horizontal line (the real axis) carrying seven filled dots. Their labels, left to right: `s₁`, `s₂ = s₄`, `t₄`, `t₂`, `s₃`, `t₃`, `t₁`.

Four semicircular arcs are drawn above the line, nested/non-interlaced:

- outermost arc from `s₁` to `t₁`;
- a second arc from `s₂` to `t₂`, lying entirely under the first;
- a small arc from `s₂ = s₄` to `t₄`, nested inside the `s₂→t₂` arc;
- a small arc from `s₃` to `t₃`, sitting between `t₂` and `t₁` and nested inside the `s₁→t₁` arc only.

Five blue labels mark the *regions* (domains) the arcs cut the half-plane into, not the arcs themselves:

- `k₀` — at the top, above the outermost arc (the unbounded outer domain);
- `k₁` — between the `s₁→t₁` arc and the `s₂→t₂` arc;
- `k₂` — inside the `s₂→t₂` arc but outside the `s₄→t₄` arc;
- `k₃` — inside the small `s₂ = s₄ → t₄` arc;
- `k₄` — inside the small `s₃→t₃` arc.

Sub-caption (a), verbatim:

> (a) An example of a diagram. Here,
> τ₀ = ∞,
> τ₁ = (s₂ − s₁) + (s₃ − t₂) + (t₁ − t₃),
> τ₂ = t₂ − t₄,
> τ₃ = t₄ − s₄,
> τ₄ = t₃ − s₃.

As LaTeX: $\tau_0=\infty$, $\tau_1=(s_2-s_1)+(s_3-t_2)+(t_1-t_3)$, $\tau_2=t_2-t_4$, $\tau_3=t_4-s_4$, $\tau_4=t_3-s_3$.

**Panel (b) — right.** The same construction reduced to a single arc. Two short horizontal line segments carry one filled dot each, labelled `s_j` (left) and `t_j` (right); a single semicircular arc joins them. Two blue labels: `w₁(j)` above the arc (the outer domain) and `w₂(j)` inside it (the inner domain).

Sub-caption (b), verbatim:

> (b) We use w₁(j) and w₂(j) to denote the Fourier variables k_m corresponding to the domains that lie on both sides of the half-circle that connects s_j with t_j.
> In Figure 1a, for example, w₁(2) = k₁ and w₂(2) = k₂ (or vice versa).

No axes, units or numeric scales appear in either panel; the horizontal line is unmarked apart from the point labels.
