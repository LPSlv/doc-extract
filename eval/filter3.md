# Filter 3: what the extractor "winning" actually throws away

`eval/multifigure.md` closed with a sentence and no number: *"filter-3 pages
that carry vector figure signal and **no** raster at all are invisible to this
whole measurement, because nothing routes them and nothing counts them."*

This counts them. **There are 4,065 of them across the 711-document base, in
409 documents, and about two thirds carry a real figure.** That is the largest
block of *lost content* measured in this router — `eval/nofigure.md`'s 1,149
wasted calls are the largest block of lost *money*, and this is its mirror
image.

It is also not affordable to fix in full: routing all of them costs **+64% of
routed image tokens and +3,911 vision calls on 5,903**. One narrow slice is
priced at +6.9% and validated out of sample; everything else here is a number,
not a proposal.

Everything below is regenerated by `eval/filter3.py`.

## Filter 3 does nothing except this

```python
pm = page_mds[i] if i < len(page_mds) else ""
if pm.count("\n|") >= 3:
    continue                              # filter 3: extractor won
```

The `continue` skips four things: the `vector_furniture` drop, `render_reason`,
the `boxed_text` drop, and `renders[i] = why`. Only the last one changes what
ships. `vector_furniture` and `boxed_text` merely append to the `dropped` audit
list, and every downstream consumer — `grid_pages`, subsumption, `cost_guard`,
`page_sigs` — reads `renders`, which is written **only** when `render_reason`
returns a reason.

So filter 3 is a no-op on every page where `render_reason` would not have
fired, and its entire functional footprint is the set of pages where it
would have. Two consequences:

- The narrowing that suggests itself — *apply filter 3 only when the page has
  no figure signal* — is not a narrowing. It is deleting filter 3.
- Filter 3 is not a small optimisation. It is the single largest suppressor in
  the router: **5,137 page renders**, against the 5,903 vision calls the router
  actually makes over the same corpora.

## Where the numbers come from

Stated first, because nine defects have been found in this repo's measurement
code and every one flattered a published number.

- **The enumeration** — `eval/filter3/firings.json`, written by
  `eval/filter3.py measure`. `route()` in that file is `harvest()`'s page loop
  with the filter-3 test behind a policy callback; every other phase (filter 1,
  filter 2, the template and box sets, `grid_pages`, subsumption, `cost_guard`,
  `drop_textonly`) is **called from `harvest.py` itself**, not re-derived.
- **The replica is diffed against the shipped code.** `measure --check` runs
  `harvest()` on every document and asserts the baseline policy reproduces its
  `items` list exactly: **0 mismatches over 709 documents**. That diff is what
  caught the `boxed_text` failure mode two sessions ago.
- **Cross-checks against artifacts this session did not write.** The baseline routes **5,903 vision calls**, which is exactly the
  post-`textonly_page` figure in `eval/nofigure.md` and exactly the sum of
  `calls` over the five `docs/benchmarks/results/*.json` files, which
  `eval/bench.py` wrote. Baseline image tokens are 5,812,236 against bench's
  5,816,924 — **0.08% apart**, the same gap `eval/multifigure.md` records
  between `cost_guard`'s truncated product and bench's real pixmap.
- **The multifigure trigger set reproduces.** Of the 134 rows in
  `eval/multifigure/index.json`, **132 appear in this enumeration** as
  filter-3 pages carrying a routed raster. The two absentees are
  `ti_lm317.pdf` pp. 9 and 12 — the pages `multifigure.md` itself identifies as
  already carrying a page render, so not filter-3 skips at all.
- **The labels** — `eval/filter3/labels.tsv`, merged from three labellers per
  page by `eval/filter3.py score`, which reads every column **by name** and
  refuses to merge unless every tag carries exactly three labels.
- **The proposed rule was priced twice by different code paths.** `cost`
  (two policies, no per-page facts) and `measure` (six policies, full
  enumeration) are separate runs over the 711 documents and agree exactly:
  6,289 calls and 6,214,583 tokens. The whole `measure` was also run twice and
  `firings.json` came out byte-identical.
- **The patch itself was diffed against this script**, which is the habit that
  caught the `boxed_text` failure mode: the patched `harvest.py` in the report
  and `route()`'s `proposed` policy produce identical item lists on 60 randomly
  drawn documents.

`measure` harvests in six subprocess shards rather than through
`harvest._harvest_all`, for the reason `eval/nofigure.md` gives: that
function's `ProcessPoolExecutor` deadlocks on this corpus.

## What the discarded pages carry

Three labellers per page, five batches each, given the page image, the
instruction in `eval/filter3/PROMPT.md`, and nothing else: no file name, no
corpus, no page number, no statement of what the answer was for. The tie-break
is set **in filter 3's favour** — "break every tie away from `figure`" —
because routing one of these pages costs money and skipping it is the status
quo.

**164 of 250 pages carry a real figure: 65.6%, 95% Wilson 60–71%.**

| label | n | share |
|---|--:|--:|
| **`figure` — a real graphic the parsed table does not convey** | **164** | **65.6%** |
| `table` — the page's only non-text content is tables | 80 | 32.0% |
| `branding` — logos, banners, rules only | 3 | 1.2% |
| `none` — prose, equations, code only | 3 | 1.2% |

Labellers were unanimous on **247 of 250**; the three splits are all
`ti_*` pages of prose and display equations under a vendor logo, where the
disagreement is `none` versus `branding` and neither is a figure.

The draw is proportional apart from the floors, so the pooled rate and the
cell-reweighted population estimate agree: **2,668 of 4,065 pages, 65.6%**.

| corpus | none | branding | table | figure | n | carries a figure |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| arxiv | 2 | 0 | 2 | 15 | 19 | 79% (57–91) |
| datasheets | 1 | 1 | 63 | 139 | 204 | 68% (61–74) |
| papers | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 50% (15–85) |
| tds | 0 | 2 | 7 | 7 | 16 | 44% (23–67) |
| pmc | 0 | 0 | 6 | 1 | 7 | 14% (3–51) |

| branch | none | branding | table | figure | n | carries a figure |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `diagonals` | 0 | 0 | 1 | 7 | 8 | 88% (53–98) |
| `curves` | 1 | 3 | 64 | 156 | 224 | 70% (63–75) |
| `dense_grid` | 0 | 0 | 6 | 1 | 7 | 14% (3–51) |
| `stroke_grid` | 2 | 0 | 9 | 0 | 11 | **0%** (0–26) |

The branch split is exactly what the routing code claims and nobody had
checked. `stroke_grid` and `dense_grid` *mean* "a ruled table the extractor
missed" — and on filter-3 pages, where the extractor demonstrably did not miss
it, they find no figures at all (0 of 11 and 1 of 7). `curves` and `diagonals`
mean "bezier artwork" and "a line chart", claims filter 3's premise says
nothing about, and they carry figures 70% and 88% of the time.

The 156 distinct documents in the labelled set include **117 that lose a
figure**. Broad, not one file.

### The comparison that makes this a defect rather than a trade-off

`eval/nofigure.md` labelled 120 `curves` pages that the router **does** route,
under the same four labels and the same tie-break direction, and **88 of them
were `figure`: 73%**. This labels 224 `curves` pages that the router
**discards** and finds **156 `figure`: 70%**.

The router pays for `curves` pages that carry a figure 73% of the time and
throws away `curves` pages that carry a figure 70% of the time. The only thing
separating the two sets is whether the extractor also parsed a table somewhere
on the page. That is not a trade-off anybody chose.

(`eval/nofigure.md` quotes the same cell as "25% waste" because it counts
`table` as a real item and this file does not. Under its definition these
pages would be 98% real — 220 of 224 — which is why the comparison above is
made on `figure` alone, on both sides.)

### Why no existing eval could have seen this

`eval/figqa_select.py` picks its candidate pages from `res["items"]` — the
routed set. Every figure-QA question, v1 through v3, is therefore drawn from
pages the router already renders. **None of the 15 question pages is in this
population**, and none could be. The 22/23 headline is not evidence about
filter 3 in either direction.

## Cost: the full fix is not affordable

Priced by driving `cost_guard()` itself — `route()` calls it, it is not
re-derived — and by applying `drop_batch_furniture` per corpus, which is what a
batch run really does. Baseline: **5,903 calls, 5,812,236 image tokens.**

| variant | calls | Δcalls | image tokens | Δ | optical/ours |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **baseline (ships)** | 5,903 | — | 5,812,236 | — | **2.25×** |
| delete filter 3 | 10,313 | +4,410 | 10,190,659 | **+75.3%** | 1.79× |
| filter 3 only for `stroke_grid`/`dense_grid` | 9,991 | +4,088 | 9,887,206 | +70.1% | 1.81× |
| filter 3 only when the page has a raster | 9,814 | +3,911 | 9,540,166 | +64.1% | 1.85× |
| both of the above | 9,559 | +3,656 | 9,307,294 | +60.1% | 1.87× |

The last column is the claim the README makes. Over these five corpora
doc-extract costs 2.25× less than reading every page optically; fixing filter 3
in full takes that to 1.79×, i.e. it spends about a third of the entire
advertised saving.

`cost_guard` **amplifies** this rather than damping it, the opposite of
`eval/multifigure.md`'s case. There the added renders were few enough that a
tripped guard acted as a ceiling; here 26–39 documents cross the boundary and
collapse into `whole_document`, which on a 45-page datasheet converts 34 calls
into 45. There are also **52–78 `over_scale_guard` flips** — documents that
newly exceed `SCALE_GUARD = 15` and so make the skill stop and ask the user.
`eval/nofigure.md` lists that class of side effect as uncounted; it is counted
here.

This is the same shape `eval/multifigure.md` found: the bill is the price of a

`eval/tds-corpus.md` reached twelve signals ago and `eval/nofigure.md` reached
four more: on a TI datasheet a page with a real figure and a page with only the
header logo have nearly the same vector content.

### One feature does separate them, and it is filter 3's own constant

`pm.count("\n|") >= 3` counts lines beginning with `|`. A markdown table with a
header, a separator rule and **one data row** produces exactly 3. So the
shipped test treats a one-row table as proof the extractor captured the page.

Sweeping that constant — `T` renders the figure-signal, raster-free pages whose
table has fewer than `T` pipe rows:

| T | fires (labelled) | figure | precision | recall | population | tokens | % of routed |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 3 (ships) | 0 | 0 | — | 0% | 0 | 0 | +0.0% |
| **4** | **23** | **20** | **87% (68–95)** | 12% | **400** | +422,238 | +7.3% |
| 5 | 59 | 50 | 85% (73–92) | 30% | 955 | +916,413 | +15.8% |
| 6 | 79 | 68 | 86% (77–92) | 41% | 1,203 | +1,192,584 | +20.5% |
| 8 | 106 | 89 | 84% (76–90) | 54% | 1,637 | +1,637,865 | +28.2% |
| 12 | 144 | 115 | 80% (73–86) | 70% | 2,268 | +2,252,957 | +38.8% |
| 20 | 194 | 146 | 75% (69–81) | 89% | 3,097 | +3,059,757 | +52.6% |
| ∞ | 250 | 164 | 66% (60–71) | 100% | 4,065 | +3,938,387 | +67.8% |

Precision is monotone and cost is linear, so there is no knee to discover —
only a budget to choose. **T = 4 is the only value whose price is in the same
order as decisions this repo has already made**, and it is the one with a
non-arbitrary justification: a header, a separator and at least two data rows.

## The slice that is defensible, priced and validated

**Rule.** Filter 3 stands whenever the page carries a raster — rendering those
pages is the swap `multifigure.md` rejected on resolution grounds, and this
must not re-import it. On a page with **no** raster, filter 3 requires
`FILTER3_ROWS = 4` pipe rows rather than 3.

In-sample: **400 pages, 180 documents, 87% precision** (95% CI 68–95, n=23
labelled), the branch mix being 374 `curves`, 12 `diagonals`, 11 `stroke_grid`,
3 `dense_grid`.

Measured cost, `cost_guard` and `drop_batch_furniture` both driven for real
(`eval/filter3/cost.json`):

| | calls | image tokens | optical/ours |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| baseline | 5,903 | 5,812,236 | 2.25× |
| **T = 4, no raster** | **6,289** | **6,214,583** | **2.20×** |
| delta | **+386 (+6.5%)** | **+402,347 (+6.9%)** | |

172 documents change. **One** `cost_guard` collapse flip (`ti_lm2576.pdf`,
34 → 44 calls, and *fewer* tokens: 39,339 → 39,225, the ceiling behaving as
`multifigure.md` describes). **13 `over_scale_guard` flips** — 13 documents
newly cross `SCALE_GUARD = 15` and will stop to ask the user. Against total
prompt tokens including text, the change is **+2.3%**.

### Out of sample

Both holdouts are disjoint from the design corpora by content hash and were
fetched in earlier sessions for earlier rules: `corpus/arxiv_holdout` (348
papers) and `corpus/pmc_holdout` (250 journal PDFs).

**The blind spot itself reproduces**, which is the first thing to check, since
a defect measured only where it was found is not a defect:

| holdout | documents | vision calls if the whole blind spot were routed | page renders the fix adds\* |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `arxiv_holdout` | 348 | 2,136 → 2,618 (**+22.6%**) | **482** |
| `pmc_holdout` | 250 | 1,131 → 1,306 (**+15.5%**) | **139** |

\* excluding pages that arrive as `whole_document` because the extra renders
tripped `cost_guard` — 50 and 57 more respectively, which is why the call
delta and the render count are not the same number on `pmc_holdout`.

**The proposed rule was then run on both, and every page it adds was labelled
blind.** The two corpora were pooled into one batch under opaque `v###` tags,
so no labeller could tell which corpus a page came from, let alone which one
the rule was designed on; `eval/filter3/holdout/vmap.json` is the only thing
that maps a tag back.

| holdout | calls | renders the rule adds | labelled | carry a figure | precision |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `arxiv_holdout` | 2,136 → 2,188 | 62 | 62 (all) | 51 | **82%** (95% Wilson 71–90) |
| `pmc_holdout` | 1,131 → 1,140 | 9 | 9 (all) | 1 | **11%** (2–44) |
| **both** | | **71** | **71** | **52** | **73%** (62–82) |

In-sample the same rule measured 87% (68–95) on 23 labelled pages. 73%
out-of-sample sits inside that interval, and the non-figures are what the
in-sample labels already predicted: journal front matter (6 of the 9 pmc pages
are publisher mastheads and boxed abstracts) and boxed listings. 70 of 71
labels were unanimous.

**Say the weakness out loud: neither holdout is the corpus this rule mostly
fires on.** 324 of the 400 in-sample pages are datasheets, and a datasheet
holdout does not exist — `eval/tds-corpus.md` records that ST, Microchip, TME
and LCSC block automated fetches. This is the same mismatch that made
`arxiv_holdout` the wrong holdout for the signature-ubiquity rule
(`eval/rejected-signals.md`). What the holdouts establish is that the rule
generalises to corpora it was not fitted on; what they cannot establish is its
precision on vendor datasheets, where four fifths of its firings are.

## Verdict

**The blind spot is real, it is large, and it is the largest content loss
measured in this repo. Do not fix it in full: that costs a third of the
advertised saving. Ship the one-row-table slice, which is the part where
filter 3's premise is not merely imprecise but false.**

Four separable statements, because they have different strengths:

1. **The count is solid.** 4,065 pages, 409 documents, enumerated
   mechanically, with the replica diffed against `harvest.py` on every
   document and three cross-checks against artifacts written by other scripts.
2. **The 65.6% is an estimate with a stated interval**, from 250 of 4,065
   pages, by three runs of one model on one prompt.
3. **The full fix is a product decision, not a measurement one.** Nothing in
   the data says whether 2,668 recoverable figures are worth 64% more image
   tokens and a third of the advertised multiplier. Geometry offers no way
   out: precision moves from 66% to at best 78% and cost falls proportionally
   with recall.
4. **`FILTER3_ROWS = 4` is the slice worth shipping**, and it is a cost
   increase — the first this repo would accept. 400 pages, 180 documents, 87%
   precision in-sample and 73% (62–82) blind out-of-sample, for +386 calls
   (+6.5%), +402,347 image tokens (+6.9%), **+2.3% of total prompt tokens**,
   and 2.25× → 2.20× against full optical. It cannot degrade an existing
   figure, because it only ever renders pages that carry no raster. What it
   *can* do is push 13 documents over `SCALE_GUARD`, which is user-visible and
   is the reason this is a judgement call rather than an obvious yes.

## The labellers are three runs of one model

They are independent of each other's answers, not of each other's blind spots.
**247 of 250 unanimous is evidence of determinism, not of reliability**: a
systematic misreading appears three times and reads as consensus. The Wilson
intervals price sampling error only; they cannot price a shared misread. The
same caveat is recorded in `eval/strokegrid.md` and `eval/multifigure.md`, and
applies with more force here, because one judgement call moves a lot of the
number: the labellers counted TI's package-outline, land-pattern, solder-stencil
and tape-and-reel drawings as figures. They are mechanical drawings and the
prompt says mechanical drawings count — but a reader who thinks a land-pattern
diagram is packaging boilerplate rather than content would get a materially
lower figure. `labels.tsv`'s `note` column carries the description for every
page, so that judgement can be re-litigated without re-labelling.

## What this does not measure

- **Whether a lost figure loses an answer.** Only `eval/figqa.md` measures
  that, and by construction it cannot see this population at all. 65.6% is
  exposure; exposure is an upper bound on harm. The page's text, caption and
  the parsed table all survive regardless — `process_pdf` runs over the whole
  document independently of routing.
- **The 769 pages carrying a raster no filter kept.** Same invisibility,
  different argument, not counted here beyond the cross-tab.
- **The 303 pages with a routed raster** beyond reproducing multifigure's
  count. Its verdict stands.
- **Whether `table`-labelled pages are really safe.** 80 pages were labelled
  `table`, and the labeller was told text and tables are recovered separately.
  If any of those pages carries a *second* table the extractor did not parse,
  it is a loss this counts as a save. Filter 3 is given the benefit of that
  doubt throughout.
- **The proposed rule's precision on datasheets**, where four fifths of its
  firings are. Both holdouts are journals and preprints. Building a datasheet
  holdout is the blocked job `eval/tds-corpus.md` describes.
- **Wall time.** The proposed rule reads `pm.count("\n|")` and the raster map
  the filter-1 loop already built, so it is free to compute, but nothing was
  timed.
- **Any corpus outside these five**, and `bills`/olmOCR in particular, where
  filter-3 prevalence is unknown.
