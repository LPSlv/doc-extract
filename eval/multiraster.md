# The multi-raster half — the cheap half, labelled, and still rejected

`eval/multifigure.md` rejected *"render the whole page when a raster fires on a
page that also shows vector figure signal"* on the **lone**-raster half: 131
items, +0.99% of routed image tokens, and a median **0.27×** linear resolution
loss on the figure the router was already reading.

It left the other half open with a cost and no verdict: the rule as worded does
not say *lone*, and on the multi-raster half it covers **308 rasters on 96
pages**, where collapsing to one render per page is **−34,044 tokens and −212
vision calls**. Cheaper *and* fewer calls — the opposite sign. `docs/NEXT.md`
called it "the one worth labelling next", on the reasoning that the resolution
argument which killed the lone half might not apply when a page already carries
several rasters that each get cropped small.

**It applies. The crops are the same size; there are just more of them.**

**Verdict: reject the swap.** It saves 0.54% of routed image tokens and 3.6% of
vision calls, it recovers a real graphic on **61.5%** of the pages it fires on —
and it pays by destroying resolvable detail *inside* the crops the router
already reads on **39.6%** of them. Twenty-six pages get strictly better, six
get strictly worse, thirty trade one figure for another, and twenty-nine change
nothing but the bill. That is the same trade the lone half was rejected for,
made at a discount rather than at a premium.

Everything below is regenerated by `eval/multiraster.py`.

## The published split reproduces exactly; its accounting does not

A fresh harvest of the same four corpora reproduces all four stored components
to the digit:

| | published | here |
|---|--:|--:|
| `multi_signal` | 308 | **308** |
| `multi_pages` | 96 | **96** |
| `multi_crop_tok` | 150,934 | **150,934** |
| `multi_render_tok` | 116,890 | **116,890** |
| delta | −34,044 | **−34,044** |
| calls | −212 | **−212** |

Two things are nonetheless wrong with it, and both flatter the change.

**Two of the 96 pages already carry a page render.** Exactly as three of the
lone half's 134 did. Their crops survive subsumption only because the same xref
is drawn on a second, unrendered page, so a render of *this* page removes
nothing and adds nothing. **Effective set: 94 pages.**

**"Rasters on this page" was counted by item page, not by placement.** An
item's `page` is `min(its placement pages)`, but `harvest.py` subsumes a raster
only when **every** page it is drawn on is rendered. The two sets differ:

| | count |
|---|--:|
| rasters whose *item page* is a candidate page | 302 |
| rasters *drawn on* a candidate page | 305 |
| rasters a collapse **really** subsumes | **300** |

One page, `r074` (`ti_tlv1117.pdf` p13), has four rasters drawn on it and
subsumes **none** — every one of them is also drawn on another unrendered page.
Collapsing it is +1,699 tokens and +1 call for nothing at all.

Priced on what is really removed:

| | tokens |
|---|--:|
| the 300 crops today | 144,043 |
| the 94 pages rendered | 114,334 |
| **first order** | **−29,709  (−0.54%)** |
| `cost_guard` second order | **0** |
| **total** | **−29,709**, and **−206 vision calls** |

The published −34,044 over-credits the change by **4,335 tokens and 6 calls**.

### The token model, stated

Identical to `eval/multifigure.py`'s, so the two halves are commensurable:
**`_tok()` on the xref's native pixel dimensions for a crop**, and
`_tok(int(w·s), int(h·s))` with `s = render_edge(pg) / long edge` for a page
render. That is what `cost_guard()` prices and what `eval/bench.py` publishes.
`eval/rejected-signals.md` records that this model *under*-prices the shipped
raster by 0.6% in aggregate because `_raster_pixmap` clip-renders the placement
isotropically. This change **removes** raster tokens, so the bias makes the
modelled saving **smaller** than the real one — it flatters the status quo, in
the direction that matters, by well under a percent.

### The denominator moved, and `multifigure.md`'s is stale

| | multifigure.md | here |
|---|--:|--:|
| vision calls | 5,922 | **5,650** |
| routed image tokens | 5,740,958 | **5,548,426** |

The difference is exactly the 272 renders `textonly_page` drops on these four
corpora. `eval/bench.py`'s regenerated JSONs agree with the figures here:
3,191 + 920 + 1,284 + 255 = **5,650** calls, `standalone_raster` 519 + 271 +
453 + 102 = **1,345** rasters, and `ours_tok − txt_tok` summed = 5,552,888
against 5,548,426 here, **0.08% apart** — the documented gap between bench
measuring a render's real pixmap and `cost_guard` truncating the product, the
same 0.08% `multifigure.md` reports. Percentages in `multifigure.md` quoted
against 5,740,958 are ~3.4% relatively low.

### `cost_guard` is exactly zero here, and that is not luck

`cost_guard()` was **driven**, per document, on both item lists with the real
`fitz` document — not re-derived. **Zero** of the 70 documents holding a
candidate page flips into `whole_document`, and none flips out.

Two structural reasons, both worth stating because the lone half's term was
−1,051 and the soft-mask case's cancelled the entire saving:

- A document that has *already* collapsed routes no raster at all, so it can
  never hold a candidate page. Only currently-routing documents can move.
- On those, the change is token-negative, and `cost_guard` fires when the
  routed set costs *more* than `whole`. A cheaper routed set can only move
  away from that boundary.

There is no `grid_pages` cascade either — the failure that made the QR-code
filter revert and that cancelled soft-mask suppression. `grid_pages` counts a
raster toward an unrendered page `Q` unless **every** page that raster is drawn
on is rendered; marking a candidate page `P` as rendered leaves `Q` unrendered,
so the condition still holds and `Q`'s tile count is unchanged. The only count
that moves is `P`'s own, and `P` is now rendered anyway. The candidate pages
could not have been `raster_grid` pages either: the most rasters drawn on any
of them is **6**, and `RASTER_GRID = 6` needs more than six.

## Why these pages are not rendered already — one mechanism, again

| | pages |
|---|--:|
| **filter 3** (`pm.count("\n|") >= 3`, "the extractor won") | **94** |
| already rendered | 2 |
| `vector_furniture` / `boxed_text` / other | 0 |

All 94. Identical to the lone half's 131-of-131. This is not a raster-routing
problem in either half: it is filter 3 discarding a page for its table while
the page's figure goes with it. `eval/multifigure.md` already says the honest
population is larger still — filter-3 pages with figure signal and **no**
raster are invisible to both measurements.

## The crux: the resolution argument does apply

For every routed raster the collapse removes, the linear scale it ends up at
inside the page render, as a fraction of the crop shipped today — the same
arithmetic as `eval/multifigure.py resolution`:

| percentile | **multi-raster (here)** | lone raster (multifigure.md) |
|---|--:|--:|
| p0 | 0.06× | 0.07× |
| p25 | 0.20× | 0.14× |
| **p50** | **0.37×** | **0.27×** |
| p75 | 0.49× | 0.53× |
| p100 | 0.95× | 0.94× |
| below 1.00× | **100%** | 100% |
| below 0.50× | **79%** | 74% |
| below 0.25× | **33%** | 45% |

292 rasters measured — the 300 subsumed less 8 for which `_raster_pixmap`
finds no page placement and falls back to the stored samples.

**Better at the median, worse at the half-resolution line, and identical where
it counts: not one raster of 292 keeps its resolution.** The hypothesis behind
this whole exercise — that several rasters on one page are individually small,
so one render at `render_edge` might serve them all — is false. The median crop
here covers **4.7%** of its page. In the lone half it covered **4.8%**. Pages
with several rasters do not carry *smaller* rasters; they carry *more* of the
same size, which is why the collapse is cheaper and why it degrades just as
much.

## The labelled benefit, and the labelled harm

94 pages, one PNG each, every crop the collapse would remove outlined in red.
Three labellers, four batches each, given `eval/multiraster/PROMPT.md` and
nothing else — no file name, no corpus, no page number, no statement of what
the answer was for. **94 pages × 3 labellers = 282 rows, two answers each, no
tag missing from any labeller and none duplicated.** Both questions break ties
**against** the change.

**These images are not rendered at 130 dpi**, and the deviation from
`eval/strokegrid_render.py` and `eval/multifigure.py` is deliberate. Those
asked only what lies *outside* the box, a question the page's own resolution
does not bear on. Half the question here is whether the replacement image still
*resolves* what is inside the boxes — and the replacement is a render at
`render_edge(pg)`, which on **81 of these 94 pages is smaller than a 130 dpi
render, median 0.71× linear, about half the pixels**. Labelling a 130 dpi image
would have asked "is this legible?" of a picture sharper than the one that
ships. Each PNG is instead exactly the pixmap `convert.py` would emit.

| | n / 91 | share | 95% Wilson |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| **recovers** a graphic outside the crops | 56 | **61.5%** | 51–71% |
| **destroys** resolvable detail inside the crops | 36 | **39.6%** | 30–50% |

Three pages are excluded and the two reasons are kept apart. `r074` has **no
rectangle by construction** — no raster on it is subsumable, so there is
nothing to reason about, and it is excluded on the code's fact, not on a label.
`r087` and `r094` do have rectangles and all three labellers reported none:
both are 24×27 pt CAD callouts, one of them drawn in red ink on a red journal
masthead. That is a **labeller** failure, not a code fact, and it is counted
separately so neither hides the other.

Crossed, which is the table the verdict rests on:

| | n / 91 |
|---|--:|
| recovers, nothing destroyed — **strictly better** | **26** |
| destroys, recovers nothing — **strictly worse** | **6** |
| both: trades a legible figure for a glimpse of another | 30 |
| neither: only the bill changes | 29 |

56 recoveries across 41 of 68 judged documents, 36 losses across 28 — broad on
both sides, not a single-file artefact.

### The labels and the pixel arithmetic corroborate each other

They were produced independently — one from a vision model reading a PNG, the
other from `get_image_info` bboxes and `_tok`:

| merged label | n | median resolution ratio |
|---|--:|--:|
| `detail` = `lost` | 36 | **0.26×** |
| `detail` = `legible` | 55 | **0.46×** |

The labellers' legibility threshold lands between 0.3× and 0.4×, which is
exactly where the median of this population sits. That agreement is the
strongest thing in this document: neither measurement had access to the other.

### By shape

Grouped by how many crops the collapse really removes — one definition
throughout, tokens and labels alike:

| crops subsumed | pages | tokens | judged | recovers | destroys |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 0 | 1 | **+1,699** | 0 | – | – |
| 2 | 47 | **+7,247** | 45 | 27 | 15 |
| 3 | 14 | −2,868 | 14 | 8 | 3 |
| 4 | 10 | −63 | 10 | 9 | 7 |
| 5 | 8 | −9,683 | 8 | 8 | 6 |
| 6 | 14 | **−26,041** | 14 | 4 | 5 |
| **total** | **94** | **−29,709** | **91** | **56** | **36** |

**Half the population costs tokens.** The 47 two-raster pages — the shape
furthest from a composite figure — are +7,247, and they recover something on 27
of 45 while destroying detail on 15. The entire net saving lives in the 5- and
6-raster tail, 22 pages, −35,724.

**Which is an independent confirmation of `RASTER_GRID = 6`.** `grid_pages`
already collapses a page tiled with *more* than six rasters, and its docstring
records the measurement that going below six "starts to eat those
distinct-figure pages … each with fine on-screen text". These 14 six-raster
pages are exactly one tile below that threshold: they recover almost nothing
(4 of 14) and destroy detail on 5. Lowering `RASTER_GRID` would buy those
tokens at that price. The threshold is where it should be.

## The trap reproduces, on the same axis

`eval/multifigure.md` warns that the cost-**negative** subset looks free and is
the worst of the set, because `render_tok ≤ crop_tok` selects the cases where
the crop is expensive, and a crop is expensive precisely when the xref is
high-resolution. Scored here:

| | pages | median resolution ratio |
|---|--:|--:|
| the collapse is token-negative on this page | 40 | **0.25×** |
| the collapse costs tokens on this page | 54 | **0.47×** |

Same relation, same size, on a different half of the same idea. The 40 pages
that carry the saving — −70,905 tokens on their own, 1.28% — are the 40 pages
that throw away the most detail.

Every trigger, priced with the labels:

| trigger | pages | crops | tokens | calls | recovers | destroys | p50 res |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| any vector figure signal (as worded) | 94 | 300 | −29,709 | −206 | 56/91 | 36/91 | 0.37× |
| signal is `curves` or `diagonals` | 84 | 268 | −19,618 | −184 | 52/81 | 32/81 | 0.39× |
| ≥3 rasters on the page | 46 | 206 | −38,655 | −160 | 29/46 | 21/46 | 0.39× |
| ≥4 rasters on the page | 32 | 164 | −35,787 | −132 | 21/32 | 18/32 | 0.39× |
| ≥5 rasters (`RASTER_GRID` 6→4) | 22 | 124 | −35,724 | −102 | 12/22 | 11/22 | 0.32× |
| the collapse is token-negative here | 40 | 156 | **−70,905** | −116 | 25/40 | 21/40 | **0.27×** |

No narrowing separates the recovery from the destruction. Every subset destroys
detail on 39–50% of its pages, and the subsets that save the most tokens are
the ones with the worst resolution ratio. There is no threshold in this table
that buys the benefit without the harm.

## The version that loses nothing, priced

| variant | tokens | share | vision calls |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| swap: render **instead of** the crops | −29,709 | −0.54% | −206 |
| additive: render **and keep** the crops | +114,334 | +2.06% | +94 |

Unlike the lone half, the additive variant here is strictly worse on both axes
than doing nothing *and* on both axes than the swap. It buys the 56 recoveries
with no degradation at all, at 2% of the token budget and 94 extra round trips.
It is the row to argue about only if the recoveries are ever shown to be worth
something — and `eval/figqa.md`, at n=23, is still the only measurement in this
repo that ties a lost figure to a lost answer.

## Out of sample: the population is real, and it is not a datasheet artefact

`corpus/arxiv_holdout` (348) and `corpus/pmc_holdout` (250) — 598 documents,
verified disjoint from all four design corpora by content hash (1,273 distinct
sha256 over 1,286 files, **zero** cross-corpus collisions):

| | design set | holdout |
|---|--:|--:|
| documents | 686 | 598 |
| pages | 15,604 | 9,930 |
| candidate pages | 94 | **73** |
| crops subsumed | 300 | **222** |
| suppressed by filter 3 | 94/94 | **73/73** |
| tokens | −29,709 (−0.54%) | **−37,906 (−1.14%)** |
| vision calls | −206 (−3.6%) | **−149 (−4.6%)** |

The trigger fires at a similar rate on documents fetched after the design set,
by the same single mechanism, for a similar saving. **The population
generalises; the verdict does not depend on it.** The holdout is *not*
labelled — a rejected rule does not need out-of-sample precision, and labelling
it would have measured the same thing twice.

## Verdict

Reject, and for the same reason as the lone half rather than a new one.

The token sign is genuinely the opposite — this is cheaper, by 0.54% of tokens
and 3.6% of vision calls, and on the holdout by 1.14% and 4.6%. But the saving
is not free and it is not even mostly beneficial: it is bought by degrading 300
rasters the router already reads correctly, to a median 0.37× of their linear
resolution, with 79% below half. Labellers judge that this destroys resolvable
detail on 40% of the pages, and their judgement tracks the arithmetic.

For scale: `textonly_page` cut 4.0% of vision calls with **zero** real items
lost across 203 blind-labelled drops, and `boxed_text` cut 0.69% with zero.
This buys 3.6% of calls at 36 damaged pages in 91. Against that bar it is not
close, and the 26 strictly-better pages do not redeem it, because the thing
being traded away is a figure the pipeline reads correctly today.

## The one second-order term left open — measured, and it is zero

This document was written before its last check finished: whether
`batch_furniture` would drop any of the page renders the swap adds, which would
change the cost. Completed afterwards over all four corpora:

| corpus | batch template | candidates matching |
|---|--:|--:|
| `arxiv` | 0 signatures | 0 / 29 |
| `datasheets` | 1 signature | 0 / 56 |
| `papers` | 0 signatures | 0 / 2 |
| `pmc` | 0 signatures | 0 / 7 |

**Zero of 94.** Only `datasheets` has a cross-document template at all, and it
matches none of the candidates there, so every cost figure above stands
unchanged.

The check is moot on the shipped path in any case, and that is worth recording
because it has now surprised two separate measurements: `convert.py` imports
`harvest()`, **not `harvest_batch()`**, so `batch_furniture` never runs for a
user at all. It fires only in `harvest.py`'s CLI batch mode. `eval/scaleguard.md`
hit the same fact from the other side — its per-corpus counts disagreed with
`eval/bench.py` on exactly `bills` and `datasheets`, the two corpora where the
batch rule fires.

## What this does not measure

- **Whether a destroyed detail loses an answer, or a recovered figure gains
  one.** Both halves of the label are *exposure*. `eval/figqa.md` (n=23) is
  still the only measurement here that ties either to a question.
- **The labellers are three runs of one model on one prompt.** 89% / 86%
  unanimity is evidence of determinism, not of reliability: a systematic
  misreading appears three times and looks like consensus. The Wilson
  intervals price sampling error only — they say nothing about that.
- **A vision model asserting "legible" is weaker evidence than one asserting
  "illegible".** The `destroys` rate is the better-supported of the two
  numbers, and it is the one the verdict rests on.
- **Filter-3 pages with figure signal and no raster.** Nothing routes them,
  nothing counts them, and they are the same defect — unchanged from
  `multifigure.md`.
- **Any corpus outside these six.** `bills`, `tds` and the six olmOCR sets are
  unmeasured, so the effect on the README's 19.9M / 6,525-call headline is
  unknown. Nothing here was regenerated into the README, because nothing here
  ships.
- **Wall time.** The trigger itself is free — `page_geometry` runs for every
  page already — but render vs. clip-render cost was not timed.
- **`over_scale_guard` flips.** Removing 206 calls moves some documents across
  `SCALE_GUARD = 15`, changing when the skill stops to ask. Not counted, the
  same gap `eval/nofigure.md` records.
