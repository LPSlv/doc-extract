This study was registered in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials.

### Results

Of 107 participants with traumatic finger injury that were recruited to the
study, seven were excluded before randomization. The age range of the
participants was 16–50 years. Fifty participants were randomly allocated to the
intervention group (ice packing) and fifty participants to the control (no ice
packing) group. None were lost to follow-up and fifty participants were analyzed
in each group [Figure 1]. Male patients comprised 92% of the participants in each
group. The mean age (SD) of the participants were 32.2 (10.3) and 30.4 (10.4) in
the ice packing and control groups, respectively. Upper limb and soft-tissue
damages were common in patients. There was no significant difference in age, sex,
injury characteristics, and baseline pain score between the two groups [Table 1].

Ice packs were applied to the site of digital nerve block before needling in the
intervention group. The NRS was recorded at "needling" and "infiltration" times
in both the groups and compared with each other [Figure 2]. The median (IQR) of
"needle stick" pain score was 1.0 (3.0) and 7.0 (1.0) in the intervention and
control groups, respectively. The median (IQR) of "infiltration" pain score was
2.0 (2.2) in the ice group and 9.0 (2.2) in the control group. Both needle stick
and infiltration pain scores were significantly lower in the intervention group.
Both needle stick and infiltration delta pain scores, according to the baseline
scores, were significantly more decreased in the ice group than the control group
[Table 2].

High satisfaction was observed in the ice packing group (38.8% vs. 0%), and the
difference of satisfaction was statistically significant (*P* = 0.001)
[Figure 3]. There was no complication in any participants of the groups.

### Discussion

#### Needle stick and infiltration pain

In this study, ice packs were applied 6 min before a standard digital nerve block
in the intervention group comprising fifty participants. The "needle stick" pain
score decreased statistically significantly (*P* = 0.0001) in the ice group, which
was similar to the results of a study performed by Hayward *et al*.[1] Hayward
*et al*. also showed that ice application was not effective in the infiltration
pain score,[1] whereas the infiltration pain score decreased significantly in our
study similar to the results of[11] and Leff *et al*.[2]
