### Body text below the figure

**III. DEVICE IMPLEMENTATIONS**

We implement the EMC in two architectures. In the first, the IDT connects directly
to a microwave feedline via two aluminum contact pads (App. A), enabling electrical
readout of the bare mechanical resonance on both silicon and sapphire substrates
(Fig. 1a–d); this stand-alone device is the subject of Secs. IV and V. In the
second, the same EMC is integrated with a high-impedance microwave resonator to
reach the strong coupling regime (Fig. 1e–i), as studied in Sec. VI.

The microwave resonator consists of a meandering NbTiN waveguide periodically
patterned with square holes [13], connected to each end of the EMC IDT through
aluminum bandages (App. B). An external magnetic field threading the holes drives
screening currents that modify the kinetic inductance, tuning the resonator
frequency as $\Delta f_\mu / f_\mu \approx -k B_{\mathrm{ext}}^2$ [24]. Its high
kinetic inductance yields a characteristic impedance
$Z_{\mu,\mathrm{tot}} = 2200\,\Omega$ in a 133 µm footprint, with a low capacitance
$C_{\mu,\mathrm{tot}} = 13.4\,\mathrm{fF}$ (App. F).

We build the microwave resonator from niobium titanium nitride (NbTiN). Its short
quasiparticle lifetime keeps the superconductivity resilient in optically intense
environments [25, 26], a prerequisite for the optical pumping needed in future
microwave-to-optics transduction. NbTiN also offers a high sheet kinetic
inductance, letting the meandering waveguide reach a characteristic impedance
$Z_{\mu,\mathrm{tot}} = \sqrt{L_\mu/(C_\mu + C_{\mathrm{IDT}})}$ above 1 kΩ
(Fig. 1e). This benefits the electromechanical coupling: at fixed resonance
frequency, a higher impedance means a lower capacitance $C_\mu$, raising the
participation of the IDT capacitance as
$g_{\mathrm{em}} \propto C_{\mathrm{IDT}}/(C_\mu + C_{\mathrm{IDT}})$.

**IV. ROOM TEMPERATURE CHARACTERIZATION**

To characterize the EMCs at room temperature we use microwave probes to connect a
vector network analyzer to the electrode pads (Fig. 3a). The devices are measured
and analyzed using microwave reflection spectrum for chips on silicon (Fig. 3b,c)
and sapphire (Fig. 3d,e). At room temperature our system is strongly undercoupled
due to the small IDT capacitance $C_{\mathrm{IDT}}$ and large material losses, so we
calibrate the measurement setup to remove background signals and enhance device
visibility. We fit the complex reflection signal $S_{11}$ to the equation [27]

$$S_{11} = 1 - \frac{2\gamma_{\mathrm{c}}}{\gamma}\,\frac{e^{i\phi}}{1 + 2i(\omega - \omega_{\mathrm{m}})/\gamma}$$

where $\phi$ accounts for impedance mismatches in the circuit, $\omega_m$ the
mechanical resonance frequency, $\gamma_i$, $\gamma_c$ and $\gamma$ the internal,
external and total mechanical loss rate, related by
$\gamma = \gamma_i + \gamma_{\mathrm{c}}$ (Fig. 3a).
