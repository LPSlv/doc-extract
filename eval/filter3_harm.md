# Filter 3: how much of the exposure is actually harm

`eval/filter3.md` measures **exposure** — *a figure exists on this page* — and
says so in its own limitations:

> **Whether a lost figure loses an answer.** Only `eval/figqa.md` measures
> that, and by construction it cannot see this population at all. 65.6% is
> exposure; exposure is an upper bound on harm.

This measures harm. **It is a small fraction of exposure, and which fraction
depends entirely on what you count as an answer.**

On 65 screened questions about figures on pages filter 3 discards, the shipped
pipeline — the page suppressed, `doc.md` alone — picks the right option
**61 times (94%, 95% Wilson 85–98)**. It can *quote the line that settles it*
only **41 times (63%)**. So:

| what "loses an answer" means | harm, as a share of pages that carry a figure |
|---|--:|
| the reader picks the wrong option out of four | **6.2%** (95% Wilson 2–15) |
| the reader cannot point at the passage that settles it | **36.9%** (26–49) |

**Those two percentages are harm as a fraction of exposure**, directly: one
question was authored per page, every page already carried a blind `figure`
label, so the denominator *is* exposure. Against the blind spot as a whole —
4,065 pages, 65.6% carrying a figure — harm is **4.0% of pages (1.6–9.7)** by
the letter measure and **24.2% (17.2–32.2)** by the grounding measure, i.e.
**164 pages (65–394)** or **985 pages (699–1,309)** of 4,065. The intervals
carry the question-sampling error only, not the 60–71% on exposure itself.

Both are far below 100%, and the gap between them is not noise — it is the
measurement's main limitation, stated in §"The multiple-choice format flatters
the status quo".

**The mechanism is exactly the one predicted.** These figures are vector
artwork, so the strings printed inside them are real text spans and survive
into `doc.md`. Split the questions by what the answer turns on, classified by
an agent that saw the page but had no stake in the answer:

| the answer turns on | n | status quo wrong | status quo cannot ground it |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **`printed`** — a legend entry, an axis label, a dimension callout | 30 | **0** (0%, 0–11) | 5 (17%, 7–34) |
| **`geometry`** — a curve's value, which trace is higher, what connects to what | 35 | **4** (11%, 5–26) | 19 (54%, 38–70) |

Every one of the four outright losses is a reading off a plotted curve:
an overshoot amplitude, a threshold voltage at the cold end of a sweep, a
thermal impedance at 1 ms, a worst-case error at 150 °C. Nothing else was lost.

Everything below is regenerated by `eval/filter3_harm.py`.

## Why the gate is not `figqa`'s gate

This is the single most important design decision here, and getting it wrong
would have produced a confident, meaningless 100%.

`eval/figqa.md` admits a question only if the text-only arm gets it **wrong**.
That is right for what figqa measures — what the visual layer *adds* on pages
the router already selects — but applying it here would force the status-quo
arm to 0/n and prove harm = exposure by construction. Three of figqa's four arm
rows are forced by its gate and it says so; here the arm under test is the one
figqa forces.

So the gate is:

```
admit iff  optical correct  AND NOT (closed-book correct under BOTH orderings)
```

The visual alone can answer it; convention alone cannot; **the text arm is left
free to succeed, because whether it succeeds is the measurement.**

Everything else mirrors figqa: four options, a recorded permutation checked for
positional bias, a second deterministic ordering for the closed-book arm, each
arm run by a separate agent given that arm's material and nothing else, and
letter-match grading with no judgement at scoring time.

## Two denominators, and why both are reported

| set | n | what it answers |
|---|--:|---|
| **sound** | 65 | every question the page settles and the screener passed. A question the closed-book arm can also answer is one the reader does **not** lose when the page is suppressed — zero harm — so for a *harm* measurement it belongs in the denominator. |
| **admitted** | 24 | additionally drops the 45 questions convention can reach, mirroring figqa. Isolates what the page *uniquely* carries. |

They agree on the shape and differ on the level, which is the honest situation:

| | n | status quo correct | grounded | fix correct | LOST | LOST (ungrounded) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| sound | 65 | 61 (94%) | 41 | 64 (98%) | **4 (6.2%, 2–15)** | **24 (37%, 26–49)** |
| admitted | 24 | 22 (92%) | 12 | 23 (96%) | **2 (8.3%, 2–26)** | **12 (50%, 31–69)** |

## The cheap slice, reported first

`eval/filter3/` already held 250 rendered, blind-labelled pages. **23 of them
are the in-sample `T = 4` set** — the pages the rationed rule would add. Three
are labelled `table`: no figure, so no question exists and no answer can be
lost. That leaves **20 pages**, one question each, authored from the render.

**The status quo answered 19 of 19 sound questions correctly. Zero lost.**
It could ground only 10 of them, so the ungrounded-loss rate is 9/19 = 47%
(27–68). Eleven of the twenty are reachable by convention alone.

That is the number most likely to move the decision, and it moved it: on the
slice the repo was about to buy, the letter-level harm is **0 of 19**
(95% upper bound 17%).

## The wider set

70 pages, seeded (`SEED = 20260814`), three strata, all drawn from pages a
blind labeller already called `figure`:

| stratum | n | drawn from |
|---|--:|---|
| `insample_T4` | 20 | census of the `figure`-labelled `T = 4` pages in `eval/filter3/labels.tsv` |
| `holdout_T4` | 30 | `eval/filter3/datasheet_holdout/labels.tsv` — 27 datasheets stratified by vendor, plus a **census of the 3 `corpus/pmc` figure pages** |
| `blindspot` | 20 | the `curves`/`diagonals` `figure` pages of the 250-page sample that `T = 4` does **not** reach, stratified by corpus |

`holdout_T4` and `blindspot` are the two populations the decision is about:
the first is the rationed rule's own out-of-sample firing set, the second is
what the full branch-gated fix would add beyond it.

Publisher front matter — the corpus `docs/NEXT.md` names as where the rule is
weakest — was deliberately sought, and it turns out it can barely enter this
eval at all. Of the 12 `corpus/pmc` firings, **8 are `branding` and 1 is
`table`**. A masthead carries no figure, so no question can be built on it and
no answer can be lost there. **Where the rule is least precise, harm is zero by
construction rather than by measurement**, and that is the strongest single
argument in the rule's favour that this eval produces: its wasted firings waste
money, not content. All 3 real pmc figure pages are in the set, and the status
quo answered all three.

- **69 candidates** after one withdrawal (below).
- **Ground truth is sound: the optical arm answered 69/69, every one grounded.**
  No question in this round was malformed — the strongest of the three
  confirmations figqa reports, and here it is unanimous across six independent
  screening agents too, which found **0 wrong answer keys in 69**.
- 4 questions **screened unfair** and dropped: `h01`, `h06`, `h31`, `h52`. All
  four give their own answer away — a stem that prints `s2 = s4` and then asks
  which points coincide; a pin literally named `VGATE` in a question asking
  which pin drives the gate.

That is **65 sound questions from 65 distinct pages**, against `figqa.md` v3's
23 admitted from **16** pages (`eval/figqa/v3/admitted.json`) — its own writeup
names small n and page clustering as its main weaknesses, and both are better
here. On the gated set it is 24, which is figqa's n, from 24 distinct pages.

**They are not 65 independent trials, and the reason is peculiar to this
population.** Two pairs are the *same drawing* reproduced in different
documents: `h03`/`h04` are both the Nexperia SOT108-1 outline, `h65`/`h66` are
both TI's DRL0005A board layout. More broadly **44 of the 65 sound questions
sit on TI or Nexperia pages**, many of them package artwork drawn to the same
house style, so one describer habit — or one convention the closed-book arm
knows — moves several at once. Five documents contribute more than one question
(different pages: `ti_opa192.pdf` three, four others two), so the 69 candidates
come from **63 distinct documents**. Effective independent units are nearer 50
than 65. That concentration is the population's, not the sample's:
`eval/filter3.md` records 3,336 of the 4,065 blind-spot pages as datasheets,
`ti_tlv9061.pdf` alone contributing 92.

## Authorship bias, and what happened when it was controlled

The threat is obvious: an author looking at a figure writes questions a picture
answers. Four controls, and one of them found something.

**1. Half the questions were authored without the picture.** Each page was
assigned a mode by seeded coin flip inside its stratum:

- **image-first** (35): the author saw the 200 dpi render and nothing else.
- **caption-first** (35): one author saw **only the page's own text layer** —
  caption, prose, whatever axis labels survived — and with no picture at all
  wrote the question *stem*. A second author then read the render and supplied
  the four options and the key. **The topic was chosen by someone who could not
  know whether the text answers it.**

The result is the opposite of the feared direction:

| authored | n | LOST | LOST (ungrounded) | share the screener called `geometry` |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| image-first | 32 | 2 (6.2%) | 9 (28%) | 15/32 |
| caption-first | 33 | 2 (6.1%) | 15 (46%) | 20/33 |

**Looking at the picture biased authors toward the questions text answers**, not
away from them — toward printed labels and callouts, which is what a figure
shows you first. The blind authors, reasoning from a caption about what a
reader would want, asked more often for the shape of the thing. Had only
image-first authoring been used, the ungrounded-loss estimate would have come
out **18 points lower**.

**2. A separate screening agent audited every question** against the page, with
no access to any extracted text, judging relevance, fairness, whether the stem
leaks its answer, and independently re-classifying `printed` vs `geometry`. It
rejected 4 and re-classified 5 — always author-`printed` → screener-`geometry`,
i.e. the authors under-counted how much their own questions turned on shape.
The screener's classification is the one used above.

**3. One question was withdrawn for author contamination**, recorded in
`withdrawn.json` rather than deleted, as figqa records v25. The image-first
author's own status note read:

> asked for ball A13 (VBUS) deliberately, since the visible part of Table 5.1
> stops at A12, so the answer must come from the ball map

That is the bias in writing: the question was chosen *because* the text could
not answer it. Found by reading the authoring agents' status reports before
scoring, not by any check in the harness.

**4. The key is balanced and was checked.** 18/17/17/17 across A/B/C/D, against
the `C`-fourteen-times-in-thirty key that nearly cost figqa ten good questions.
The second ordering is a rotation, so no option keeps its position. No arm's
answer distribution is skewed: the widest is the closed-book arm at 21/21/11/16.

## The closed-book arm is very strong, and that is a finding

| arm | ordering 1 | ordering 2 | correct under both |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| closed-book | 51/69 (74%) | 48/69 (70%) | **45/69 (65%)** |

Random is 25%. Guessing right under both orderings is 6%. **Two thirds of
natural questions about these figures are answerable with no document at all.**

Most of it is real convention, and the arm names it: TSSOP pitch is 0.65 mm by
definition, `HE` is the overall lead span, `+IIK` clamps to `VCC`, J-STD-020
caps the cooling ramp at 6 °C/s. A large share of the "figures" in this blind
spot are standardised package outlines, land patterns, solder stencils and
tape-and-reel boxes — the exact class `eval/filter3.md` flags as the judgement
call that moves its own number:

> a reader who thinks a land-pattern diagram is packaging boilerplate rather
> than content would get a materially lower figure

This is the quantitative version of that caveat: on these pages the drawing is
largely a picture of a standard, and a reader who knows the standard loses
little when it is suppressed.

**Do not read 74% as "a human would also get 74%".** The closed-book agent and
the question authors are runs of the same model, so its priors are the
distractor-writer's priors, and a "plausible near-miss" written by a model is
recognisable to that model. Some of the 24 correct-but-self-declared-guesses
are that artifact rather than knowledge. The `admitted` column exists precisely
so a reader who distrusts this can use a number that does not depend on it.

## The multiple-choice format flatters the status quo

Four options with a balanced key means guessing scores 25%, and the status-quo
arm returned **21 answers that were correct but which it could not ground** —
against about 6 expected from blind guessing. Those 21 are not the text
recovering the figure; they are inference from spec tables, physics and
convention, narrowed by having four options in front of it.

figqa found the same thing and named it: *"Guessing counted as recovery… its
ungrounded answers ran 11/17 correct against ~4.2 expected."* It chose multiple
choice deliberately, because free-text answers get graded by string match and
stray tokens in the extracted text falsely credit the text arm. That trade is
right, and this is its price.

So **6.2% is the floor on harm and 36.9% is the ceiling**, and an open-ended
question — the way a user actually asks — sits somewhere between, probably
nearer the ceiling, because none of the narrowing that multiple choice provides
is available. Nothing here pins it down. That is the single biggest thing this
measurement does not do.

## What the fix buys

The doc-extract arm — the same `doc.md`, plus a description written by another
agent from the page rendered at exactly the edge the **shipped** renderer would
choose for it (`harvest.render_edge`, cross-checked against
`eval/filter3/index.json` on all 70 pages, 0 mismatches) — scores **64/65
(98%), 62 of them grounded**.

It recovers **3 of the 4 losses**, all three curve readings. The fourth, `h29`,
is a transient-thermal-impedance value at 1 ms where the log-log curves have
nearly merged; the describer transcribed the plateau and the far end but not
the asked point, and said so. That is the same shape as figqa v3's one genuine
miss: the page was seen, and the specific reading still was not made.

On grounding the gain is much larger than on letters: **41 → 62 of 65**. What
the render buys is mostly not *the answer*; it is *the citation*.

## Harm as a fraction of exposure, and what it implies

Exposure and harm compose. Pages where an answer is actually lost =
population × P(figure) × P(answer lost | figure).

| | rationed: `FILTER3_ROWS = 4`, branch-gated | full branch-gated fix |
|---|--:|--:|
| pages added | ~400 | 3,792 (`curves`/`diagonals`, no raster) |
| calls | +374 | +3,656 |
| image tokens | +6.9% | +60.1% |
| total prompt tokens | **+2.3%** (measured) | ≈+20% (scaled from that ratio, not measured) |
| optical/ours | 2.25× → 2.20× | 2.25× → 1.87× |
| P(figure), measured | 85% (78–89) | 70% (64–76) |
| P(answer lost \| figure), **letter** | 6.4% (2–17), n=47 | 5.6% (1–26), n=18 |
| P(answer lost \| figure), **ungrounded** | 40% (28–55) | 28% (12–51) |
| **pages where an answer is recovered, letter** | **~22** | **~154** |
| **pages where a citation is recovered, ungrounded** | **~137** | **~797** |

The full-fix column is computed as the union it really is: its ~400 `T = 4`
pages at the `T = 4` rates, and the remaining ~3,392 at the `blindspot` rates.

Read the two rows at the bottom against the two cost rows at the top.

**The rationed rule buys about 22 recovered answers for +2.3% of prompt
tokens** — or about 137 recovered citations, if you count a reader who guesses
right without being able to cite as a loss. Both are cheap. Its price is
already inside the range this repo has accepted for other reasons, and the
harm measurement does not argue against it. It does not argue strongly *for* it
either: 22 answers across 711 documents is not a large number, and the honest
case for the rule now rests on the citation figure, not the answer figure.

**The full branch-gated fix buys roughly 154 recovered answers for +60% of
image tokens** — about a third of the advertised 2.25× multiplier, for one
recovered answer per 25 rendered pages. `eval/rejected-signals.md` rejected it
on price when the benefit was priced in exposure at 2,668 recoverable figures.
Priced in harm the benefit is an order of magnitude smaller and the price is
unchanged. **Nothing in this measurement rescues the expensive option; it makes
the case against it considerably stronger.**

The asymmetry between the two rows is the useful part. Between them the
rationed rule and the full fix differ by a factor of **9.8 in cost** and a
factor of **7.1 in recovered answers** — but the full fix's marginal pages
are *worse*, not better: 70% carry a figure against 85%, and their loss rate is
no higher.

### The counter-argument, stated fairly

If the product's promise is citation rather than recall — and the README's is,
since the whole skill exists to produce *citable* Markdown — then the
ungrounded column is the right one and the rationed rule recovers ~137 pages
of citable content for +2.3%. That is a good trade and is the strongest case
for shipping `FILTER3_ROWS = 4` gated to `curves`/`diagonals`. It is still not
a case for the full fix, whose marginal pages are less likely to carry a figure
at ten times the price.

## Defects found in this harness

Assume one, go looking. Two were found, and neither by a test.

**1. The doc-extract arm was run against three empty description directories.**
`docx-b3` covered `h34`, `h35`, `h36` while the describer for those pages was
still running; the arm answered from `text.md` alone and *reported it* —
"`mat_docx/h34/`, `h35/` and `h36/` are empty directories". This is figqa v3's
withheld-routed-items defect reproducing exactly: **the fix arm was scored
without the material the fix provides**. Caught by reading the arm's own status
report. Those three were re-run against the real descriptions. None of them can
move the headline — all three text answers are correct, so they enter neither
the loss count nor the recovery count — but the defect did understate the fix's
grounding (61 → 62 of 65) and left one unverifiable quote behind, on `h35`,
which `verify` then flagged.

**2. One question was authored to be text-unanswerable**, `h33`, described
above. That is a defect in the instrument, not in the code, and it is the one
this eval was most at risk from. It was found in an authoring agent's prose,
which no check reads.

Both were found by reading what the agents said about their own work rather
than by anything the harness computes. That is the lesson: the arms report
their own problems, and nothing in the pipeline was looking.

### The checks that passed

- **`verify` traces every grounding claim to the arm's own material.**
  `uv run eval/filter3_harm.py verify` confirms the passage each grounded
  answer quotes really occurs in the file that arm was given. **All 44 grounded
  text-arm claims and all 66 grounded doc-extract claims** do. That is also the
  leakage check: had the text arm read a page render, its quote would not be in
  its markdown.
- **`score` asserts the key against the sheet the arms actually saw** — that the
  answer is still `options_raw[0]`, that both keys index the answer in their own
  ordering, that the option *sets* match, and that the second ordering really
  moves the answer. figqa's fourth harness defect was a scorer reading the wrong
  column; this refuses to score at all if the columns drift.
- **A cross-check against a file this session did not write.** `render_edge()`
  from the shipped `harvest.py`, run over all 70 pages, reproduces the `edge`
  column stored in `eval/filter3/index.json` with **0 mismatches**. The
  describer therefore saw the resolution the fix would really produce.
- **Positional bias**: key 18/17/17/17, and no arm's answer distribution is
  skewed toward a position (widest is closed-book at 21/21/11/16).

The structural leakage control is that each arm has its own directory —
`mat_text/` holds no images, `mat_optical/` holds no markdown — so an arm
cannot reach another arm's material by listing its own. That is not a
guarantee; the arms have general file access and only an instruction stops them
wandering. figqa has the same exposure and does not state it.

## The arms are runs of one model on one prompt

Six describers, six screeners, six text-arm agents, six doc-extract agents,
six optical agents and two closed-book agents — all the same model, all
independent of each other's *answers*, none independent of each other's *blind
spots*. Where they agree, that is determinism, not reliability: the optical
arm's 69/69 and the screeners' 0-of-69 wrong keys are evidence that the
questions are well formed under one reader, and a systematic misreading would
appear in every arm at once and read as consensus. `eval/filter3.md`,
`eval/strokegrid.md` and `eval/multifigure.md` record the same caveat; it
applies with extra force here because the closed-book arm's 74% is partly the
authoring model recognising its own distractors.

The Wilson intervals price sampling error only. They cannot price a shared
misread, and they cannot price the multiple-choice format.

## What this does not measure

- **Free-text answering**, which is how the tool is actually used. Multiple
  choice is the whole reason 94% and 63% are so far apart. This is the first
  thing to fix.
- **`doc.md` as literally shipped.** The status-quo arm is `process_pdf`'s
  whole-document markdown, matching figqa's text arm. The real `doc.md` also
  carries descriptions of items routed on *other* pages, so the arm is very
  slightly understated and harm very slightly overstated. Producing the real
  artifact means a full vision pass over 65 documents.
- **Non-figure pages.** 34% of the blind spot carries no figure at all and
  contributes zero to both exposure and harm; 3 of the 23 `T = 4` pages and 9
  of the 12 `corpus/pmc` firings are in that class. They are counted here as
  "no question exists", which is right, but it means the pmc cell — the rule's
  worst — is measured by construction rather than by experiment.
- **How much a reader would have wanted the answer.** The screener rated
  relevance high on 49 of 69 and medium on 19, but a question nobody asks
  costs nothing when it is lost, and that is not priced.
- **The 769 filter-3 pages carrying a raster no filter kept**, and the 303 with
  a routed raster. Same populations `eval/filter3.md` names and does not touch.
- **Whether the describer would hold up at scale.** 65 descriptions, written by
  agents told to follow `reference/describing-visuals.md`, are not a production
  vision pass, and one of them (`h29`) already failed to make the reading asked.
- **Wall time and money.** Neither the describers nor the arms were timed.
