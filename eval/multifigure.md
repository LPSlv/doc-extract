# Multi-figure pages — priced on both sides, and rejected

`eval/rejected-signals.md` left this open with a sentence rather than a
verdict: *"An obvious fix — render the whole page whenever a raster fires on a
page that also shows vector figure signal — would convert 134 crops into 134
page renders. That is a cost increase for a defect measured at 1 in 23, and it
has not been justified. It should be measured properly before anyone
implements it."*

This is that measurement. It is not close, and it does not fail for the reason
anyone expected.

**Verdict: reject the swap.** It costs +0.99% of routed vision tokens, it
recovers a real, previously invisible figure on **half** the pages it fires on
— a much bigger exposure than the 1-in-23 headline suggests — and it pays for
that by shrinking the figure it *already* reads to a median of **0.27× linear
resolution**. It buys a second figure by half-blinding the first. The variant
that loses nothing — render the page *and* keep the crop — costs +2.13% and
131 extra vision calls, and was never what was proposed.

Everything below is regenerated by `eval/multifigure.py`.

## The split reproduces exactly, and its stated denominator does not

| | published | here |
|---|--:|--:|
| routed rasters | 1,345 | **1,345** |
| on pages with >1 raster | 728 (54.1%) | **728 (54.1%)** |
| lone raster, page also shows vector figure signal | 134 (10.0%) | **134 (10.0%)** |
| genuinely the only figure on the page | 483 (35.9%) | **483 (35.9%)** |
| documents | *1,014* | **686** |

Corpora are `datasheets`, `pmc`, `arxiv`, `papers` — 688 files, 2 unreadable,
686 harvested, 15,604 pages, 5,922 vision calls. Those counts match
`docs/benchmarks/results/{datasheets,pmc,arxiv,papers}.json` exactly, and those
files were written by `eval/bench.py`, not by this one. The **1,014** in
`rejected-signals.md` is wrong; every count downstream of it is right, which is
how it survived.

*Vector figure signal* had to be recovered rather than read off, since nothing
defined it. It is `render_reason(page_geometry(pg)) is not None` — the router's
own predicate — and only that definition reproduces 134. Alternatives were
tried and do not: `curves>=8 or diagonals>=4` gives 217, `_plot_shaped` alone
gives 234, restricting to the two bezier branches gives 120.

## Why a routed page ever fails to be rendered — one mechanism, not several

If `render_reason` fires, the page is normally rendered and the raster on it is
subsumed. So every one of these 134 is a page where something suppressed the
render *before* `render_reason` ran. There are four candidates in `harvest()` —
filter 3, `vector_furniture`, `boxed_text`, and the OCR branch.

**All 131 are filter 3.** `if pm.count("\n|") >= 3: continue` — "the extractor
won". A page carrying both a parsed table and a vector figure has its render
skipped on account of the table, and the figure goes with it.

That reframes the item. This is not a raster-routing problem; it is filter 3
discarding a page for one reason while ignoring another reason to keep it. It
also means the honest population is larger than 134 and unmeasured: filter-3
pages that carry vector figure signal and **no** raster at all are invisible to
this whole measurement, because nothing routes them and nothing counts them.

### Three of the published 134 are not losses at all

`m042`, `m043` (`ti_lm317.pdf` pp. 9, 12) and `m048` (`ti_lm386.pdf` p. 6) sit
on pages that **already carry a page render**. Their crops survive subsumption
only because the same xref is also drawn on a second, unrendered page, and an
item's `page` is the smallest of its placements. The fix would change nothing
for them. `ti_lm386` p6 is the same page `eval/figqa.md` records as having had
a page render withheld by a harness bug — an independent confirmation from the
other direction.

**Effective trigger set: 131.** The cost below is quoted for 131; the labelled
benefit for the 129 of those that are judgeable.

## Cost, and which model it uses

**The model is `_tok()` on the xref's native pixel dimensions for a crop**, and
`_tok(int(w·s), int(h·s))` with `s = render_edge(pg) / long edge` for a page
render. That is exactly what `cost_guard()` prices and what `eval/bench.py`
publishes, so the delta is commensurable with the README's totals. Denominator:
5,740,958 routed image tokens over the four corpora — `eval/bench.py`'s own
figure for the same set is 5,745,683, 0.08% apart, the difference being that
bench measures a render's real pixmap while `cost_guard` truncates the product.

| | tokens |
|---|--:|
| the 131 crops today | 67,280 |
| the same 131 pages rendered | 124,911 |
| first order | **+57,631** |
| `cost_guard` second order | −1,051 |
| **total** | **+56,580  (+0.99%)** |

Against the README's 20.1M headline across all thirteen corpora it is +0.28%.

**The bias, measured rather than named.** `rejected-signals.md` records that
`_tok(*it["px"])` underprices the shipped raster by 0.6% in aggregate and up to
58% per item, because `_raster_pixmap` clip-renders the placement isotropically.
The swap *removes* raster tokens, so that bias inflates the modelled cost of
the change. Pricing all 131 from the PNGs `convert.py` really writes:

| | crops | renders | delta |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| modelled | 67,280 | 124,911 | +57,631 |
| shipped | 67,642 | 125,062 | +57,420 |
| model error | +0.5% | +0.1% | −0.4% |

So the model overstates the increase by 0.4% of itself. It flatters the status
quo, in the direction that matters, by an amount too small to change anything.

### `cost_guard` damps this one; it amplified soft-mask suppression

Two documents cross the boundary and collapse into `whole_document`:

| | pages | routed today | routed after | whole | calls |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `ti_lm2576.pdf` | 45 | 39,339 | 40,246 | **39,884** | 34 → 45 |
| `AJPS-20-243.PMC10450116.pdf` | 2 | 1,422 | 2,715 | **2,026** | 3 → 2 |

Both were confirmed by driving `cost_guard()` itself on a hand-built item list,
not by re-deriving its arithmetic: it returns the collapse with
`routed_tokens` 40,246 / 39,884 and 2,715 / 2,026, the same numbers.

The sign is the opposite of the soft-mask case. There, dropping mask xrefs took
pages below `RASTER_GRID` and *un-collapsed* renders into crops, and the saving
cancelled. Here `cost_guard` is a **ceiling**: a document that trips it pays
`whole`, which is by construction less than the routed set that tripped it. So
the second-order term is −1,051 tokens. It does cost **+10 vision calls**,
because collapsing a 45-page datasheet turns 34 calls into 45.

## Benefit: 65 of 129 pages really do lose a figure

131 pages rendered at 130 dpi (mirroring `eval/strokegrid_render.py`), each
with the region `_raster_pixmap` actually clips outlined in red. One image
carries both facts a judgement needs — what is sent, and what surrounds it — so
no crop can be paired with the wrong page.

Three labellers, four batches each, given the images and the instruction in
`eval/multifigure/PROMPT.md` and nothing else: no file name, no corpus, no page
number, no statement of what the answer was for. Ties broken **toward
`sufficient`**, i.e. against the change.

**65 of 129 judgeable pages lose figure content the crop does not carry.**
95% Wilson interval **42–59%**.

Two pages are excluded, and *why* they are excluded is the point. On `m015` and
`m114` `_raster_pixmap` finds no page placement and falls back to the stored
samples, so no rectangle is drawn and there is no region on the page to reason
about. They are unjudgeable **by construction**. All three labellers said
`norect` on `m114`; on `m015` one said `norect` and two answered `loses`,
reading the figure's own red ink as the marker. Excluding these on the code's
fact rather than on the label is what keeps that judgement-about-nothing out of
the numerator.

Those 65 come from **59 distinct documents** out of the 91 in the judged set.
This is not the "free win" shape — six pages of one file — that
`rejected-signals.md` warns about. It is broad.

| corpus | loses | of |
|---|--:|--:|
| arxiv | 14 | 19 |
| papers | 5 | 7 |
| datasheets | 45 | 89 |
| pmc | 1 | 14 |

| signal | loses | of |
|---|--:|--:|
| `diagonals` | 6 | 7 |
| `curves` | 58 | 108 |
| `dense_grid` | 1 | 6 |
| `stroke_grid` | **0** | 8 |

`stroke_grid` costs 8 renders and recovers nothing — one more measurement in
this repo saying what `eval/strokegrid.md` already says about that branch.

**The labellers are not three labellers.** They are three runs of one model on
one prompt over the same images. 129 of 131 unanimous is evidence of
determinism, not of reliability: a systematic misreading appears three times
and looks like consensus. The same caveat applies to `eval/strokegrid`'s
holdout and is worth saying out loud once.

**And "loses figure content" is not "loses an answer."** The 1-in-23 in
`eval/figqa.md` is the only number here tied to a question someone could not
answer. This one measures exposure, and exposure is an upper bound on harm:
`text.md` still carries the missed figure's caption and the prose around it,
and figure-QA v3 found several questions answered that way. Read 50.4% as *how
often a second figure exists outside the crop*, not as *how often the pipeline
gets something wrong*.

## What actually kills it: the crop you already had

The labelling asks what a page render would **add**. It cannot see what the
same render takes **away**, and `_raster_pixmap` exists precisely because that
is not nothing. Its own comment:

> Preserve the image's own resolution: scale so the rendered region is about
> its native pixel count. Capping this at a fixed multiple of the *placement*
> silently degrades a high-resolution image that happens to be placed small,
> which is common for schematics and scope captures.

A page render caps at the placement by construction. The median trigger crop
covers **4.8%** of its page. So for every one of the 129 measurable triggers,
the linear resolution of the raster that is *already being read* after the swap,
as a fraction of the crop shipped today:

| percentile | ratio |
|---|--:|
| p0 | 0.07× |
| p25 | 0.14× |
| **p50** | **0.27×** |
| p75 | 0.53× |
| p100 | 0.94× |

**100% below 1.00×. 74% below 0.50×. 45% below 0.25×** — a quarter of the
linear resolution is a sixteenth of the pixels. On the 64 pages where the
labellers found nothing outside the crop, that degradation is paid for nothing
at all.

This is the whole argument. The proposal is not "spend 1% to see more". It is
"spend 1% to shrink the figure you can already read to a quarter of its linear
size, in exchange for a glimpse of a second one — and half the time there is no
second one".

## Narrower triggers do not rescue it, and the one that looks free is the worst

Every trigger scored against the same 129 labels, with `cost_guard` re-applied
per document to each subset:

| trigger | fires | gets | recall | precision | tokens | per figure |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| any vector figure signal (the proposal) | 129 | 65 | 100% | 50% | +53,431 | +822 |
| signal is `curves` or `diagonals` | 115 | 64 | 98% | 56% | +52,363 | +818 |
| crop covers <30% of the page | 123 | 65 | 100% | 53% | +53,211 | +819 |
| `curves`/`diagonals` **and** crop <30% | 111 | 64 | 98% | 58% | +53,014 | +828 |
| render costs at most 1.5× the crop | 36 | 13 | 20% | 36% | **−11,435** | −880 |
| render costs no more than the crop | 21 | 7 | 11% | 33% | **−13,822** | −1,975 |

(The proposal row is 129 rather than 131 because two pages are unjudgeable; the
headline cost above is for all 131.)

Narrowing barely moves the bill. Excluding `stroke_grid` and `dense_grid` drops
14 firings and one figure and saves 1,068 tokens — 2% of the cost — because the
bill is dominated by the price of a page render, which is roughly constant per
page and does not care why the page was selected. There is no threshold here
that isolates the losses cheaply; the cost is close to all-or-nothing.

The two cost-**negative** rows are the trap. "Render costs no more than the
crop" removes 13,822 tokens *and* recovers 7 figures: strictly better on both
axes, 19 distinct documents, no single-file artefact. It reads exactly like the
free win `rejected-signals.md` had to retract.

It is not free. `render_tok ≤ crop_tok` selects the cases where the crop is
*expensive*, and a crop is expensive precisely when the xref is high-resolution.
Median resolution ratio in that subset is **0.21×**, against 0.34× for the rest
— the apparently-free trigger discards the most detail in the set. It is cheap
because of what it throws away.

The same relation holds across the labels: pages labelled `loses` sit at a
median 0.24× and pages labelled `sufficient` at 0.35×. The crops most likely to
hide a neighbour are the crops that suffer most from being replaced.

## The version that loses nothing, priced

| variant | tokens | share | vision calls |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| swap: render **instead of** the crop | +56,580 | +0.99% | +10 |
| additive: render **and keep** the crop | +122,441 | +2.13% | +131 |

The additive variant is the only form of this fix that does not trade one
figure for another. It is 2.2× the price, it adds 131 vision calls to 5,922
(+2.2%), and nobody has proposed it. If this idea is ever revisited, that is
the row to argue about — not the swap.

## The rule as literally worded covers a much larger set, with the opposite sign

"When a raster fires on a page that also shows vector figure signal" does not
say *lone*. Read literally it also fires on the multi-raster half: **308 routed
rasters on 96 pages**. Collapsing those into one render per page costs
150,934 → 116,890 tokens, **−34,044**, and **−212 vision calls**.

That is a saving, not a cost, over a set more than twice as large as the 131 —
308 of the 1,345 routed rasters, 23%. It is unlabelled, so there is no benefit
estimate and no verdict here — but it is the larger and cheaper half of the
idea, and it is the one nobody costed. `RASTER_GRID = 6` already does exactly
this above six tiles; what this says is that the same collapse is
token-negative well below six when the page also carries vector figure signal.
The resolution objection above applies there too and would have to be measured
the same way.

## What this does not measure

- **Whether a lost figure loses an answer.** Only `eval/figqa.md` measures
  that, at n=23, and only one of its questions is in this class.
- **Filter-3 pages with figure signal and no raster.** Nothing routes them,
  nothing counted them, and they are the same defect.
- **Legibility after the swap.** The resolution ratio is arithmetic on pixel
  counts. Nobody looked at a degraded render and judged whether the schematic
  is still readable; the threshold at which 0.27× stops mattering is unknown.
- **The multi-raster half's benefit.** Cost only.
- **Wall time.** The trigger itself is free — `page_geometry` already runs for
  every page and `render_reason` is arithmetic over its result — but the render
  vs. clip-render cost was not timed. Soft-mask suppression died on 40 ms/doc;
  nothing here approaches that, but it is unmeasured.
- **Any corpus outside these four.** In particular `bills`, `tds` and the six
  olmOCR sets, where the trigger's prevalence is unknown.
